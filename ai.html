The same saving, under two pricing arrangements

On hourly terms, a task properly completed and checked in two hours should not be billed as four because it once took four. The invoice falls. Under an unchanged and fair fixed fee the practice keeps the agreed fee and bears the cost of the tool.

Sensitivity to review time

Checking is the variable that decides whether the saving is real. The longer the review, the less time is released.

Three things this screen does not say.

Time saved is not revenue earned. Additional collections are not profit, because the work that fills the time has its own cost. An AI-assisted draft still requires professional review, and no figure in this tool suggests what a sufficient review would be. The review time here is an assumption you choose, not a professional standard.

What the courts require in Jamaica. The Supreme Court's Practice Direction No 1 of 2025, effective 17 September 2025, requires AI-assisted court documents to be verified and disclosed by the prescribed declaration. Subject to its express terms it prohibits using generative AI to draft or alter affidavits or witness statements meant to express personal knowledge or belief, to generate purported expert evidence or to fabricate evidence. The Caribbean Court of Justice's Practice Direction No 1 of 2025 also requires verification and care with confidential and privileged material.

Comparative guidance elsewhere, including the Law Society of England and Wales practice note of 21 October 2026, rejects charging an hourly client for time a task would have taken without AI. That is not Jamaican law. For Jamaican practice the starting point remains Canon IV(f), accurate time records where time is the basis of charging and the written engagement.

The price of a tool is more than its subscription. Count set-up, training, checking, mistakes, downtime, security and the cost of leaving it. A decision to buy should also settle which tools are approved, how client confidentiality is protected and who supervises the output.
