What the practice keeps

Contribution is the fee you collect less the cost of delivering the work. It is not profit: it has not yet paid for the overhead of matters that never came in, tax, financing or bad debts.

An attractive model does not make a fee proper. Whatever this screen shows, the fee must be fair and reasonable under Canon IV(f) in the actual circumstances: the time and labour, the difficulty, the skill required, the customary fee for similar work, the amount involved, the urgency, your experience and reputation and whether the fee is fixed or contingent.

A fixed fee is not fully earned merely because a retainer ends early. Unearned sums must be accounted for. A fixed fee must never reward thin service, and clients with much at stake and limited means need particular care in matching scope and price to what they actually need.

Before you quote a fixed or staged fee, settle these in writing.

  • Scope. What the stage includes, deliverable by deliverable.
  • Assumptions. What must hold true for the price to stand: records supplied by a date, one claimant, no new party.
  • Exclusions. Appeals, further applications, experts, extra hearings.
  • Authorisation. Each stage approved in writing before it starts, and a priced process for changes of scope.
  • Termination. How you will account if the engagement ends mid-stage, subject to the stage fee as a ceiling and to the fee being fair and reasonable.
  • Disbursements and GCT. Named, estimated and approved, and stated to be outside the fee.

The staged fixed-fee clause from Appendix B

A fixed fee does not guarantee the cost of contested litigation. Where the opponent, the court or the facts drive the work, price the stage in front of you and price the next one when you reach it.
