The clause

Our fee. Our fee is fixed for each stage of the work. You will know the price of a stage before we start it.

Stage 1: Protect and assess (J$300,000). We will file the acknowledgment of service, review the claim and your records and give you a written assessment of your exposure, your options and the strategy we recommend.

Stage 2: Defend and negotiate (J$700,000). We will prepare and file your defence and, where there is a proper factual and legal basis, a counterclaim; undertake the agreed negotiation and represent you at the first case management conference.

Choosing the stages. Stages 1 and 2 together cost J$1,000,000. All three stages together would cost J$1,350,000 on the stated assumptions. You may stop after any authorised stage and you pay nothing for a later stage you have not agreed.

Optional Stage 3: Mediation (J$350,000). If you choose this stage, we will prepare for and attend one mediation session on the assumptions in the Schedule. We will start only on your separate written instruction.

Payment and billing. You pay each stage fee before the stage begins. We hold any advance for future work in our clients' trust account under the Accounts and Records Regulations. We transfer it only against fees properly earned and billed or as the law otherwise permits.

The agreed price. The stage fee does not rise because the work takes us longer than expected and does not fall because it takes less. It pays for the defined professional services and our judgment. It does not guarantee the outcome of the dispute. Changes of scope and early termination are dealt with below.

What the fee assumes. You will deliver the records identified in the Schedule within 14 days. There is one claimant and no new party or claim is added. Negotiation involves written exchanges and up to two meetings. Other assumptions and exclusions appear in the Schedule.

Changes to scope. If an assumption fails, a new application or party is introduced or you ask for more work, we will explain the effect on scope and price in writing and ask for your approval before we proceed. The only exception is a step we are professionally bound to take at once or one the court requires.

Later stages. The quoted prices for optional Stages 2 and 3 remain available for acceptance during the validity period specified in the Schedule and on the stated assumptions. Each stage requires separate written authorisation before work begins. Any further stage will be scoped and priced when reached. An agreed fee for a stage already authorised will not be changed because its work takes longer than expected.

What is not included. The stage prices exclude General Consumption Tax (if chargeable), court fees, filing charges, expert fees, counsel's fees outside the agreed team and other disbursements. We will identify and seek approval for material disbursements before incurring them, subject to urgent professional or court obligations.

If the engagement ends during a stage. We will account for work properly performed at the agreed hourly rates set out in the Schedule, subject to the agreed stage fee as a ceiling and to the requirement that the resulting fee be fair and reasonable. We will deliver a bill and statement of account and return any advance not properly earned, in accordance with the applicable rules. The stage fee is not automatically earned merely because the engagement ends.

Review. We keep a record of the time spent so that the fee can be explained or reviewed if you ask.

Schedule, to complete before signing

  • Client and matter
  • Stage authorised now, and the date of written authorisation
  • Scope and deliverables: documents, advice, applications, meetings and any attendance limits
  • Assumptions: number of parties, records to be provided, deadlines and expected court steps
  • Exclusions: appeals, additional applications, experts, additional hearings, unexpected parties
  • Disbursements: items, estimate, approval arrangements and payment responsibility
  • GCT: applicable treatment and whether added to the stated fee
  • Optional-stage price validity, subject to the stated assumptions
  • Hourly rates for agreed extras and for a fair accounting on early termination, agreed and completed before signature, never imposed retrospectively
  • Signatures and dates

The measures, defined once

  • Revenue. Fees billed in the period. It does not tell you whether they will be paid or what the work cost.
  • Accounting profit. Fees billed less overhead, before the owner's pay. It misses the cost of the owner's own labour and the timing of cash.
  • Cash available. Money in the office account after payments made. It misses liabilities due next month and work not yet billed.
  • Sustainable owner income. What the owner can take out year after year without starving the practice. Tax, reinvestment and bad years still need judgment.
  • Cost per billable hour. Total cost of running the practice, including a market allowance for the owner's fee-earning work, divided by realistic billable hours.
  • Realisation. Fees billed as a share of recorded value. Meaningful for hourly work. Do not apply it to fixed fees.
  • Collection rate. Fees collected as a share of fees billed, followed through the same cohort of invoices.
  • Debtor days. Unpaid bills divided by average daily billings.
  • Lockup. How long the firm waits between doing the work and being paid, counting unbilled work and unpaid bills together. Debtor days is the unpaid-bills part.
  • Contribution. Fees received for a matter less the staff cost, direct expenses and a fair share of the overhead that produced them. It is not profit.

Jamaican law and practice directions

Binding in Jamaica

  • Legal Profession Act, ss 21, 22 and 35. Section 21 deals with written remuneration agreements, section 22 with the taxation of bills. Laws of Jamaica, Ministry of Justice.
  • Legal Profession (Canons of Professional Ethics) Rules, 1978, as amended. Canon IV(f) makes fairness and reasonableness the governing test for a fee, with time and labour one factor among several, alongside difficulty, required skill, customary fees, the amount involved, urgency, experience and reputation and whether the fee is fixed or contingent. See also Canon IV(a), (b), (g), (n) to (p) and Canon VII. The 2019 amendment rules added the obligations on sole practitioners to protect client property.
  • Legal Profession (Accounts and Records) Regulations, 1999, in the General Legal Council consolidation through 2017, with the 2019 amendment. Further amendments were gazetted on 4 June 2021 and 28 July 2021, effective 1 January 2022, and are published with the rest on the Council's legislation page. These govern client money and the accounting records a practice must keep.
  • Legal Profession (Contingency Fees) Regulations, 2018, effective 1 February 2019, regs 4 to 13. A signed written agreement is required and ceilings of 25%, 30% and 33⅓% apply depending on the stage reached.
  • Russell (Trudy-Ann) v General Legal Council [2024] JMCA Misc 2, Court of Appeal, 17 May 2024, at [33] and [46] to [72].
  • McGlashan (Winsome) v Nicholson Phillips [2022] JMCC Comm 36, Supreme Court, Commercial Division, claim SU2020CD00252, delivered 9 December 2022.
  • Supreme Court Practice Direction No 2 of 2018, assessment of costs, 31 January 2018. Published with the others on the Supreme Court's practice directions page.
  • Supreme Court Practice Direction No 1 of 2025, use of generative artificial intelligence in court proceedings, effective 17 September 2025.
  • Caribbean Court of Justice Practice Direction No 1 of 2025, 14 February 2025.
  • General Legal Council Annual Report 2022 to 2023, the source of the practising certificate figures used in the paper.

Citations follow the paper. Every link goes to the official publisher: the Ministry of Justice, the General Legal Council, the Court of Appeal, the Supreme Court or the Caribbean Court of Justice. Links were checked on 19 November 2026. Legislation is amended and pages move, so verify the current text and status of any provision before relying on it in a matter.

Comparative material, not Jamaican law

Comparative

  • Law Society of England and Wales, practice note on using artificial intelligence in legal practice, 21 October 2026. On time-based fees it says only time actually and properly spent may be billed. Under other models it expects a clear scope and a fee that remains justified.
  • Virginia Legal Ethics Opinion 1901, reasonable fees and the use of generative artificial intelligence, approved by the Supreme Court of Virginia on 24 November 2025. It takes a different route from the Law Society: the opinion states that Rule 1.5 does not equate reduced time with proportionally reduced fees, so a lawyer who works faster with AI is not required to cut the fee where the client still receives the value of the work. The reasonableness of the fee remains the test.
  • Queensland Law Society, Guidance Statement, Artificial Intelligence in Legal Practice, published 31 May 2024. The Society now lists it as Guidance Statement No 37, though the published PDF bears the number 38.
  • Survey and market evidence from the United States, the United Kingdom and the wider Caribbean, discussed in the paper with its sample sizes and limits stated.

None of this binds a Jamaican practitioner, and the comparative material does not speak with one voice. England and Wales approaches AI efficiency through time actually spent. Virginia approaches it through the value the client receives. The paper treats the debate as a foreshadowing of a conversation the profession here will have, and Canon IV(f) remains the Jamaican starting point either way.

Links checked on 19 November 2026. The Law Society restricts automated access to its site, so that address is taken from the practice note's own published link rather than opened directly; if it moves, search the Law Society site for the title.

What the figures in these tools are

  • Every default is a hypothetical teaching assumption from the paper. None is a Jamaican market rate, and no Jamaican fee scale exists.
  • The worked examples are reproduced from Appendix C and are tested against the paper's printed figures every time this site is built.
  • Amounts are before tax unless stated, and exclude disbursements.
  • The cash-flow model is a simplified cash-timing model, not a profit and loss statement.
  • The AI model assumes payroll is unchanged and that replacement work uses existing capacity. A practice that must add staff or supervision should enter that cost.
  • No benchmarks are offered. Compare your results with your own history.

Your figures and your clients' information

Everything you type is calculated in your own browser. Nothing is transmitted, stored on a server or sent to any AI service. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics and no tracking. Closing the tab clears what you entered.

These tools never ask for a client's name, a client account number, a description of a matter, a privileged document or any banking credential. Do not type any of those into this or any other web page you do not control.

The outputs are illustrative financial estimates for educational use. They are not legal, accounting or tax advice. No disclaimer substitutes for your own judgment on a particular matter.
